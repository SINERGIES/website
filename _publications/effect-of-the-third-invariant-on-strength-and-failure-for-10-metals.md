This article examines the effect of the third invariant on the strength and failure characteristics of 10 metals. The diverse range of materials includes Armco iron, OFHC copper, cartridge brass, carpenter electrical iron, nickel 200, 1100 aluminum, 1006 steel, 4340 steel, 6061-T6 aluminum and a tungsten alloy. Quasi-static stress-strain data are presented for both tension and torsion tests and the results are put into an equivalent (von Mises) stress versus equivalent strain framework such that meaningful comparisons can be made. For strength effects the torsional data tend to fall below the tensile data (for a given equivalent strain), and the difference tends to increase as the strain increases. For failure it is much more difficult to isolate the effects of the third invariant as the failure can be dependent on both the mean stress and the third invariant. Discussions are provided to quantify the effects of the third invariant for computational models, and to provide guidance on determining constants for these models.
