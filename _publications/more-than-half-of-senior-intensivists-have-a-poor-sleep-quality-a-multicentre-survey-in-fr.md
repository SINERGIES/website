Background: Intensivists face an elevated risk of sleep disorders. We hypothesized a relationship between poor sleep quality and stress at work among intensivists. The purpose of this survey was to assess the prevalence and factors associated with poor sleep quality among French intensivists. Methods: Cross-sectional survey among French intensivists from 38 ICUs between November 2024 and February 2025. Anonymous, online, self-administered questionnaire was completed by participants. Sleep quality was evaluated by Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index (PSQI) and perceived stress intensity at work measured by the Perceived Stressors in the Intensive Care Unit scale (PS-ICU scale). The main outcome was poor sleep quality defined as a global PSQI score >5. A multivariate logistic regression analysis was performed. Results: 217 intensivists completed the questionnaire (response rate = 55%). The prevalence of poor sleep quality (PSQI >5) was 58%. Median [IQR] PSQI was 6 [4-8]. By multivariate analysis, we identified three independent factors associated with poor sleep quality: perceived stress intensity at work measured by the PS-ICU scale, psychostimulants use after 4:00 p.m., and excessive daytime sleepiness measured by Epworth scale (score >10). Conclusion: More than half of senior intensivists have poor sleep quality. We demonstrated a relationship between poor sleep quality and perceived stress intensity at work measured by the PS ICU scale. Strategies aimed at reducing stress at work in the ICUs, at individual or organizational levels, should be developed.
